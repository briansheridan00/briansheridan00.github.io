Welcome

I am a PhD student researching gravitational waves at the University of Nottingham. Since obtaining a master's of physics at ETH Zürich I have pursued research projects at the intersection of general relativity, computational science and statistical modelling, such as gravitational wave mode analysis for extreme-mass-ratio-inspirals (EMRIs). I enjoy using both computational and theoretical techniques to better understand and model astrophysical systems, where I am most interested in gravitational waves from binary black holes and the astrophysical environments surrounding them.

Research Interests

My research goals focus on better understanding the gravitational wave emission from binary black hole systems and their surrounding astrophysical environments, especially in regimes of high mass asymmetry. In addition to deepening our theoretical understanding of these binaries, I aim to develop comprehensive computational tools and machine learning pipelines to model and investigate these systems.

My most recent project investigated the relevance of various gravitational wave modes used in the generation of waveforms by the FastEMRIWaveforms (FEW) package, widespread in use across the scientific community. I analysed the modal coverage across parameter space and suggested the most effective way to extend modal space to better cover the possible parameter combinations, providing a clear understanding of the structure and extent of the possible excited modes.
